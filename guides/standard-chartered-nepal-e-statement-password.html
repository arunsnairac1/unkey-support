Standard Chartered Nepal e-Statement Password (2026)

Standard Chartered Bank Nepal · Nepal · Updated October 2026

As of October 2026, Standard Chartered Nepal's e-statement FAQ says the account e-statement password is the 7 digits in the middle of your account number, and the credit card e-statement password is your date of birth (DDMMYY) followed by the last 8 digits of your card number. For example, Sita Gurung (made-up), born 9 August 1991, with card 4123-5678-1234-8765, would type 09089112348765 for her card statement.

The password format

Standard Chartered Nepal's e-Statement FAQ says statements are emailed to your registered address as an encrypted PDF, and you need the password every time you open the attachment. It gives two default passwords.

Bank account e-statements

The password is the 7-digit block that forms part of your account number. The FAQ shows it as: account number 01 XXXXXXX 12, password XXXXXXX. In other words, drop the first two digits and the last two digits and use the seven in between.

Credit card e-statements (primary cardholder)

The password combines two things:

  1. Your date of birth as day, month and two-digit year (DDMMYY)
  2. The last 8 digits of your credit card number

The bank's own example: born 25 Feb 1970, card number 9876-5678-4567-1234, password 25027045671234. That is 250270 followed by 45671234, 14 digits in total.

Worked example

Sita Gurung (made-up):

  • Savings account 01 2345678 90: account statement password 2345678
  • Born 9 August 1991, so DDMMYY is 090891
  • Credit card 4123-5678-1234-8765, so the last 8 digits are 12348765
  • Credit card statement password: 09089112348765

If the password doesn't work

  • Account vs card. The two passwords are completely different. Check which statement you are opening.
  • Two-digit year. The card password uses a two-digit year (1991 becomes 91), with the day first. Use leading zeros: 9 August is 0908.
  • Eight card digits. Use exactly the last eight digits of the card number, without spaces or dashes.
  • Calendar. Nepal uses both Bikram Sambat (BS) and Gregorian (AD) dates. The FAQ's example uses an AD date. If an AD date fails, ask the bank which date of birth is on your record.
  • Supplementary cards. The FAQ only describes the primary cardholder's password. If you hold an add-on card, check the email the statement came with.
  • Old PDF reader. The bank says to use Adobe Acrobat Reader 6.0 or later for account statements and 8.1.2 or later for card statements. A very old reader may refuse the file even with the right password.
  • Still stuck? Read the email the statement arrived with, or visit your nearest branch.

Not getting statements?

The bank says to make sure your mailbox has space and to add Global.E-Statement-MESA@sc.com and eStatement.nepal@sc.com to your contacts so the emails don't land in spam. You can change your registered email address at a branch.

Removing the password so you don't have to type it again

Questions

What is the Standard Chartered Nepal account statement password?

The 7 digits that form the middle of your account number. For account 01 XXXXXXX 12, the password is XXXXXXX.

What is the credit card statement password?

Your date of birth as DDMMYY followed by the last 8 digits of your card number, for example 25027045671234 for someone born 25 Feb 1970 with a card ending 4567-1234.

Why won't my Standard Chartered e-statement open?

Check you are using the right password for the statement type, use a two-digit year for card statements, and try an up-to-date PDF reader.

Which email address do Standard Chartered Nepal statements come from?

The bank lists Global.E-Statement-MESA@sc.com and eStatement.nepal@sc.com. Add both to your contacts to avoid spam filtering.
