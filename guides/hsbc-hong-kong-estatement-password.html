HSBC Hong Kong eStatement Password Format (2026)

As of October 2026, HSBC Hong Kong protects emailed eStatements with a 9-character preset password unless you have set your own: the last digit of your birth month and the last 2 digits of your birth year, then the first 3 characters of your HKID or passport number, then the last 3 digits of your mobile number. For example, Chan Ka Yan (made-up), born 9 March 1992, HKID Y987654(3), mobile 6123 4567, would type 392Y98567.

The password format

HSBC's Online Banking FAQ says that if you haven't set up your own password, your files are protected with a pre-set password made of three parts:

  1. Part 1: the last digit of your month of birth, then the last 2 digits of your year of birth (3 characters)
  2. Part 2: the first 3 alphanumeric characters of your Hong Kong Identity Card (HKID) or passport number
  3. Part 3: the last 3 digits of the mobile number HSBC has on record

HSBC's own example: born 17/12/1985, HKID A123456(7), mobile 98785432. December is month 12, so the last digit is 2, and the year gives 85. The HKID gives A12 and the mobile gives 432. HSBC writes the result as 285 A12 432. The spaces are only there to make it easier to read. The password itself is 9 characters: 285A12432.

Only the last digit of the month is used. January (01) and November (11) both give 1, and October (10) gives 0.

HSBC also says it will ask you to change this password the first time you log on to view eStatements or eAdvices. If you have already set your own eStatement password, use that one instead.

Worked example

Chan Ka Yan (made-up):

  • Born 9 March 1992: month 03 gives 3, and the year gives 92, so Part 1 is 392
  • HKID Y987654(3): the first 3 characters are Y98
  • Mobile 6123 4567: the last 3 digits are 567

Password: 392Y98567.

If the password doesn't work

  • Have you set your own password? Once you change it under "Change Password" in the eStatement and eAdvice section, the preset no longer applies.
  • Use the month's last digit only. Typing 12 for December or 03 for March is a common mistake.
  • Use the HKID letter. Count the letter at the start of your HKID as one of the 3 characters. Type it in capitals, as HSBC's example does.
  • Old mobile number. Part 3 uses the mobile number in HSBC's records. If you changed your number recently, try the old one.
  • Older files after a change. HSBC's business eStatement FAQ says the latest password opens documents issued in the last 24 months, and that eStatements and eAdvices share one password. HSBC's personal FAQ doesn't say whether the same applies to personal accounts.
  • Skip the password. You can view and download eStatements in HSBC Internet Banking or the HSBC HK App. Check the notification email for the bank's instructions too.

Questions

What is the HSBC Hong Kong eStatement preset password?

Nine characters: the last digit of your birth month and the last 2 digits of your birth year, the first 3 characters of your HKID or passport number, and the last 3 digits of your mobile number.

Do I type the spaces shown in HSBC's example?

No. HSBC writes it as 285 A12 432 to make it easier to read, but the password is 9 characters, so type 285A12432.

I was born in December. What is Part 1?

Use only the last digit of the month, so December gives 2, followed by the last two digits of your birth year, for example 285 for 1985.

Can I change the HSBC eStatement password?

Yes. Use "Change Password" in the eStatement and eAdvice section of HSBC Internet Banking. HSBC asks you to change the preset the first time you view eStatements online.
