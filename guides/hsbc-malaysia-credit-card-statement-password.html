HSBC Malaysia Credit Card Statement Password (2026)

HSBC Bank Malaysia / HSBC Amanah · Malaysia · Updated October 2026

Your date of birth as ddMmmyyyy (e.g. 28Aug1977) followed by the last 6 digits of your HSBC credit card number.

To open an HSBC Malaysia credit card statement PDF, type your date of birth as ddMmmyyyy, followed by the last 6 digits of your credit card number. For example, someone born on 28 August 1977 with card number 4921 0000 0012 3456 would type 28Aug1977123456.

The password format

As of October 2026, HSBC Malaysia's Bank Statement FAQs say: "The password required to open your eStatements/email statements is your date of birth in ddMmmyyyy format, followed by the last 6 digits of your credit card number." The answer sits in the credit card/-i section of that page, so it covers HSBC and HSBC Amanah credit card statements.

The password has two parts, with no spaces between them:

  • Date of birth as ddMmmyyyy: two-digit day, then the first three letters of the month in English with only the first letter capitalised, then the four-digit year. 5 March 1992 becomes 05Mar1992.
  • Last 6 digits of your credit card number: the final six digits of the 16-digit number on the card.

That makes 15 characters in total. The same FAQ also says that passwords are not needed to open eStatements. It doesn't say which channel that line covers. Most likely it means statements you view after logging in to HSBC online banking. The password matters for the PDF that arrives by email or that you have saved to your phone.

Worked example

Tan Mei Ling (made-up) was born on 9 November 1985. Her HSBC Visa card number ends in 1234 5678.

  • Date of birth as ddMmmyyyy: 09Nov1985
  • Last 6 digits of the card number: 345678

Password: 09Nov1985345678.

If the password doesn't work

  • Check the capitals. Only the first letter of the month is a capital: Aug, not AUG or aug.
  • Keep the leading zero on days 1 to 9: 09Nov1985, not 9Nov1985.
  • Use the full year. This format takes four digits, such as 1985, not 85.
  • Use the right card number. If your card was replaced (after a loss, an upgrade or fraud), the statement may use the number of the card that account is under now. Try the card in your wallet first, then the old one. HSBC's FAQ doesn't say whose details apply to supplementary cards, so check the statement email.
  • Date of birth on record. Use the date of birth HSBC has for you. If it is wrong in HSBC's records, contact HSBC to fix it.
  • Read the email. The statement email normally gives the password hint, and that email is the final word if HSBC changes the format.
  • Log in instead. You can download the same statement from HSBC online banking or the HSBC Malaysia app.

Questions

What is the password for my HSBC Malaysia credit card statement?

It is your date of birth as ddMmmyyyy followed by the last 6 digits of your credit card number, for example 28Aug1977123456.

Is the HSBC Malaysia statement password case-sensitive?

Treat it as case-sensitive. Write the month as three letters with only the first one capitalised, for example Aug.

Do I need a password to view statements in HSBC online banking?

HSBC's FAQ says passwords are not required to open eStatements. The password applies to the PDF statement sent by email or saved to your device.

My card was replaced. Which card number do I use?

Try the last 6 digits of your current card first, then your old card. If neither works, check the statement email or download the statement from HSBC online banking.
